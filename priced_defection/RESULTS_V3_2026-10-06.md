# Priced Defection — v3 results

*Kolmogorov, 2026-10-06. Harness `tournament3.py` (v1 and v2 left frozen).
40 seeds/cell, 119 cells + 2 mechanism probes. Every cell carries a 95%
percentile-bootstrap CI over seeds (B=600).*

This pass worked the six-item fix list from `.swarm/handoff_priced_defection.md`.
Headline: **item 6 overturned my own v2 result.** The "one near-peer at 1.2×
substitutes for a whole coordinated collective" number is real as a mechanism
and wrong as a number. Details in §7.

## 1. Do movers resist each other? This is the load-bearing premise of the whole multipolar case

`mover_resist` ∈ {`full`, `idle`, `none`}: does a rival power contribute to the
response against another rival's grab? `full` = yes (it is invested in the
order). `idle` = only when not itself mobilising (two-front problem). `none` =
never (a precedent for grabbing is worth more to it than the order).

Pooled response (`sum`), success rate:

| movers | full | idle | none |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.28 [0.15,0.42] | 0.30 [0.15,0.45] | 0.28 [0.15,0.42] |
| 2 | **0.00** [0,0] | **0.00** [0,0] | 0.50 [0.35,0.68] |
| 4 | **0.00** [0,0] | **0.00** [0,0] | **0.82** [0.70,0.93] |

No coordination (`max`):

| movers | full | idle | none |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 0.97 | 0.97 | 1.00 |
| 4 | 0.75 | 0.78 | 1.00 |

Three things here.

**(a) The single binary that decides whether multipolarity is stabilising.**
Under pooling, if rivals defend the order, more rivals ⇒ *perfect* stability
(0.00 at 2 and 4 movers). If they don't, more rivals ⇒ near-certain grab (0.82
at 4). That is the widest swing anywhere in the parameter space, and the RFC
does not contain the parameter. It has to become a stated premise.

**(b) `idle` ≈ `full` under pooling is a self-fulfilling deadlock, not a bug.**
I probed it (`probe_deadlock.py`) because identical columns look like a coding
error. The two-front exemption is evaluated at a moment when nobody has yet
committed — so every mover counts every other mover as a live resister, so
nobody commits, so nobody is ever busy, so the exemption never fires. It is a
fixed point of the belief rule.

Confirming test: plant one reckless actor (threshold θ=0, grabs whenever p>0).

```
idle  4×EV                 att=0.00  succ=0.00
idle  3×EV + 1 reckless    att=0.30  succ=0.00  ruin=1.000
full  3×EV + 1 reckless    att=0.30  succ=0.00  ruin=1.000
runs where any mover reached execution, idle/4EV: 0/40
```

The reckless actor does break the deadlock — and is destroyed every single time
(ruin 1.000), and the EV-rational movers *still* never move. So the deadlock is
not fragile to one defector from it. Breaking it requires a mover whose
commitment survives long enough to occupy the others, which in this harness
means winning. **Caveat I am not comfortable with:** under `idle` the mover is
shown the others' current prep state, but preparation is supposed to be covert.
That is generous to the exemption, and it is the assumption most worth
revisiting.

**(c) Under `max`, more movers ⇒ *fewer* successes** (0.97 → 0.75 at `full`),
because when only the strongest detector resists, the strongest detector is
increasingly likely to be a fellow mover. Multipolarity substitutes for
coordination. This is the one place where the two mechanisms interact rather
than just adding.

## 2. Capability spectrum, confound removed

Pool total now held fixed at the reference total (selftest: 19.8745 at every
`cap_spread`, gini moving 0.054 → 0.542), so `cap_spread` moves **shape only**.

| cap_spread | gini | succ (`sum`) | succ (`max`) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.1 | 0.05 | 0.33 [0.20,0.47] | 1.00 |
| 0.3 | 0.17 | 0.28 [0.15,0.42] | 1.00 |
| 0.6 | 0.33 | 0.23 [0.10,0.35] | 1.00 |
| 1.0 | 0.54 | 0.15 [0.05,0.28] | 1.00 |
| 1.5 | — | 0.12 [0.03,0.23] | 0.88 |

Unnormalised (the v2 version), for contrast: 0.33 → 0.05.

So: **direction holds, magnitude roughly halves.** A more unequal pool of the
same total strength does deter grabs under pooling — concentration raises the
chance that the detectors who happen to see it include someone big. But the
0.1-vs-0.3 and 0.3-vs-0.6 steps are inside each other's CIs; only the
0.1-vs-1.0 endpoints separate. Under `max` inequality is irrelevant until it is
extreme, which makes sense: `max` already only reads the top of the
distribution.

## 3. Confidence intervals — and what they delete

CIs are ±0.13 to ±0.15 wide on success at 40 seeds in the mid-range. That kills
a lot of what v1 and v2 said:

- **Safe to assert:** every 0.00-vs-1.00 contrast. §1's multipolarity swing, the
  detection-dominates-speed cliff, the rival-ratio cliff, the ruin-floor effect.
- **Not safe:** anything in the 0.2–0.5 band compared against anything else in
  the 0.2–0.5 band. `tft` 0.28 vs `alld` 0.30 (§5), cap_spread 0.1 vs 0.3,
  σ=0.5 vs σ=1.0 from v1. All noise.

I am not going to raise seed counts to rescue those. If a contrast needs more
than 40 seeds to show up it is too small to carry a clause in the piece.

## 4. The cooperation finding was survivorship, start to finish

v2 reported cooperation *rising* in grab-heavy cells (0.675 → 0.756) and I
flagged it as probably survivorship. It was, completely. Three metrics now:

| cell | coop (v2 metric) | coop_H (censored, round<10) | reached_H |
|---|---|---|---|
| baseline, pooled | 0.664 | 0.742 | 0.82 |
| `max`, 4 movers, none | 0.773 | **n/a** | **0.00** |
| 4 movers, none, pooled | 0.758 | 0.777 | 0.30 |
| no grabs (2 movers, full) | 0.636 | 0.741 | 1.00 |

In the highest-grab cells **no run survives to round 10 at all**, so the
censored rate is undefined — the honest answer is that there is no cooperation
record to speak of, not that cooperation was high. Where it is defined, it is
flat at 0.74–0.78 across the entire range.

**Correct statement: a successful grab does not change how anyone behaves. It
ends the game.** Any welfare claim in the piece has to be about the truncation,
not about conduct. The v2 phrasing would have supported exactly the wrong
reading.

## 5. H4 (forgiveness) — now testable, and it fails

v2 could not test this: movers played TFT as cover and were essentially never
sanctioned. `mover_pd="alld"` makes the mover actually defect and actually get
punished. Verified it worked — `punish_frac` (share of partners whose last move
against the mover was D, at the moment it commits) goes **0.203 → 0.381**.

| mover conduct | forgiveness | succ | t_first | punish_frac |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| tft | on | 0.28 [0.15,0.42] | 8.9 | 0.203 |
| tft | off | 0.28 [0.15,0.42] | 8.9 | 0.203 |
| alld | on | 0.30 [0.17,0.45] | 13.0 | 0.381 |
| alld | off | 0.30 [0.17,0.45] | 13.0 | 0.381 |

**Forgiveness does nothing.** Identical to three decimal places with it on and
off, under both conducts and both response rules. H4 as stated is dead: in this
harness the grab decision is driven by the capability comparison and the
continuation value, and the sanctioning regime's *shape* never enters.

One anomaly worth a probe before the writeup: punished movers grab **later**
(t1 13.0 vs 8.9), not earlier, which is backwards if punishment collapses the
continuation value. Likely mechanism — an ALLD mover's early payoffs are T=5
(it is exploiting cooperators successfully), so its estimated `v_hat` is *higher*
than a TFT mover's for the first stretch, and it has more to lose. Punishment
only collapses `v_hat` after retaliation accumulates. If that holds, it is a
nice result in its own right and sharpens §2: **successful exploitation is
itself a brake on escalation.** Stated as a hypothesis, not a finding — one
probe would settle it.

## 6. Mixed decision-maker populations

Only reachable under `mover_resist="none"` (§1 explains why). Four movers:

| population | succ | ruin |
|---|---|---|
| threshold θ=0.9 ×4 | 0.07 [0.00,0.17] | 0.000 |
| log-utility, 1% ruin floor ×4 | 0.10 [0.03,0.20] | 0.200 |
| patient (δ=0.999) ×4 | 0.38 [0.23,0.53] | 0.286 |
| 2 EV + 2 threshold | 0.57 [0.42,0.72] | 0.324 |
| mixed patience (2 imp + 2 pat) | 0.68 [0.53,0.82] | 0.341 |
| impatient (δ=0.5) ×4 | **0.90** [0.80,0.97] | 0.200 |

**H2 (patience) replicates in the mixed population and is the most robust
single effect in the whole study** — impatient 0.90 vs patient 0.38, CIs
cleanly separated, and it survived every fix in this pass. Mixing patience
levels lands between the pure populations but closer to the impatient end
(0.68), i.e. **the impatient member sets the pace.** That is the practically
important version of H2: you do not get the patient equilibrium by having
mostly patient actors.

Log utility with a 1% backup still only reaches 0.10 against EV's 0.82 — so the
v2 finding that a 1% backup destroys the ruin deterrent is true *relative to
absorbing ruin* (0.00 → 0.10), but log-utility actors remain far more cautious
than EV actors at the same odds. Both statements need to be in the piece; v2
only had the first.

## 7. The nearest-competitor number was mine, not the model's

This is the correction I care most about. v2's headline — and what I told you
was the most policy-relevant thing in the space — was that one near-peer at
**1.2×** suppresses grabs where an entire uncoordinated collective does not.

### 7a. Full grid: the mechanism is scale-invariant

`rival_ratio` × `response_rule` × `cap_scale`, success rate (72 cells):

| rule | r=0.0 | r=0.8 | r=1.0 | r=1.2 | r=1.5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `max`, scale 0.8 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 0.35 | 0.03 | 0.00 |
| `max`, scale 1.0 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 0.35 | 0.03 | 0.00 |
| `max`, scale 1.3 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 0.35 | 0.05 | 0.00 |
| `max`, scale 1.6 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 0.35 | 0.05 | 0.00 |
| `topk` (3), scale 1.0 | 1.00 | 0.17 | 0.00 | 0.00 | 0.00 |
| `sum`, scale 1.0 | 0.28 | 0.00 | 0.00 | 0.00 | 0.00 |

The cliff sits in the same place at **every** cap_scale — which is the good
news, and the reason the mechanism is worth putting in the piece. It is not an
artifact of where I pinned the mover. Coordination orders cleanly: `sum` needs
a rival of 0.8×, `topk`-3 needs ~1.0×, `max` needs ~1.2×.

### 7b. But the 1.2 is a quantile of the mover's own strength, not a constant

Two probes. First, vary detection (`probe_margin.py`), margin = smallest
rival_ratio driving success ≤0.05:

| d | p_det | margin |
|---|---|---|
| 0.05 | 0.098 | >1.5 |
| 0.2 | 0.360 | >1.5 |
| 0.5 | 0.750 | 1.2 |
| 0.9 | 0.990 | 1.2 |
| 1.0 | 1.000 | **1.2** |

My hypothesis was that the 20% premium over parity is the price of imperfect
detection — the rival must both *see* the grab and *exceed* the mover. Half
right: below d≈0.36 no feasible near-peer margin exists at all. But the margin
does **not** move between d=0.5 and d=1.0 despite p_det going 0.75 → 1.00. At
perfect detection a parity rival still loses 30% of the time. So something else
sets the floor.

It is the mover's own capability jitter — drawn as `gcap · exp(N(0,σ_j))`, with
σ_j = **0.1**, a constant hardcoded in v1 that I never chose deliberately and
never declared. Promoted it to a parameter and swept it (`probe_jitter.py`,
d=1.0):

| σ_jitter | margin | exp(2σ_j) |
|---|---|---|
| 0.00 | 1.0 | 1.000 |
| 0.05 | 1.1 | 1.105 |
| 0.10 | **1.2** | 1.221 |
| 0.20 | 1.4 | 1.492 |
| 0.40 | 2.0 | 2.226 |

The margin tracks **exp(2σ_j)** — the ~+2σ quantile of the mover's own strength
distribution — to within the grid resolution at four of five points (the
σ_j=0.4 cell is the one that misses, and the grid there is coarse: 1.7 → 2.0).
At σ_j=0 the margin is exactly 1.0: a parity rival wins every time, as it must.

**So the claim has to be restated.** Not "a near-peer needs to be 20% stronger."
Rather: *a single near-peer can substitute for an entire uncoordinated
collective, provided it covers the upper tail of the mover's own capability
distribution — roughly the +2σ quantile — and provided detection exceeds a
floor of about 0.36.* The 1.2× is downstream of a modelling choice of mine. The
quantile rule is the invariant.

This also makes the result *more* useful, not less: it says the deterrence
requirement is set by how much uncertainty there is about the mover's peak
capability, which is an empirically estimable quantity, rather than by a magic
ratio.

## 9. What I'd now change in the RFC, added to the v1/v2 list

1. **Add `mover_resist` as a stated premise.** It is the widest-swinging
   parameter in the study and currently absent. The piece's multipolar
   intuitions are not entailed by its own model.
2. **Restate the near-peer requirement as a quantile of the mover's capability
   distribution**, with the detection floor as a precondition.
3. **Declare `mover_jitter`.** An undeclared constant was silently setting a
   headline number. My error, worth recording as a method lesson.
4. **Drop any welfare claim that leans on cooperation rates.** §4 — grabs
   truncate the record, they do not change conduct.
5. **Cut H4** or redesign it around something other than forgiveness.
6. Promote the "impatient member sets the pace" version of H2 — it is the
   policy-relevant form and stronger than what §2 currently claims.
