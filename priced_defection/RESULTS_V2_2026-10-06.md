## Q1a. Was uncertainty in the SELF-capability estimate incorporated?

**No. You caught a real gap.** v1 used `ag.cap` exactly — the mover had *perfect*
self-knowledge and noise only on others. All "uncertainty" in the 2026-10-06
results was purely relative. Now added as `sigma_self`.

It matters, and it is the **stronger** of the two channels:

| σ_self | σ_rel | attempts | success | ruin | believed p | realised | calib gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.0 | 0.0 | 0.60 | 0.38 | 0.375 | 0.629 | 0.625 | +0.004 |
| 0.0 | 0.5 | 0.62 | 0.33 | 0.480 | 0.596 | 0.520 | +0.076 |
| 0.1 | 0.0 | 0.65 | 0.40 | 0.385 | 0.576 | 0.615 | −0.040 |
| 0.3 | 0.0 | 0.82 | 0.40 | 0.515 | 0.506 | 0.485 | +0.021 |
| 0.5 | 0.0 | **0.93** | 0.42 | **0.541** | 0.504 | 0.459 | +0.045 |
| 0.5 | 0.5 | **0.95** | 0.35 | **0.632** | 0.479 | 0.368 | **+0.111** |

Reading: self-noise raises **attempts** (0.60 → 0.95) and **ruin** (0.375 → 0.632)
while leaving **success roughly flat** (~0.33–0.42). It does not make movers more
dangerous; it makes them *more willing to die*. Compare the two pure channels at
equal magnitude: σ_self=0.5/σ_rel=0 gives 0.93 attempts, versus σ_rel=0.5/σ_self=0
at 0.62. **Misjudging yourself produces more doomed grabs than misjudging the
opposition.**

The calibration gap (believed p minus realised frequency) is positive and grows
with total noise, peaking at +0.111. That is the overconfidence mechanism made
measurable, and it is what the v1 σ-cuts-both-ways result was actually picking up.

Design consequence, and it is a new one: §4.3 argues the collective should invest
in σ by concealing its capacity. But the channel that generates the most failed
grabs is the mover's **self**-estimate, which the collective does not control.
A mover that cannot audit its own capability is more reckless, not more deterred.

## Q1b. Is there a nearest-competitor level that prevents a grab?

**Yes, and this is the strongest result in v2.** The threshold is ~1.2×.

v1 always pooled every detector's capability (`sum`), which silently assumed
perfect coordination inside τ — the exact thing §7 lists as an unproven premise.
I added `response_rule`: `sum` (perfect pooling), `max` (no coordination — only
the single strongest detector resists), `topk` (the k strongest pool).

Planting one near-peer rival at `rival_ratio` × the mover's capability, with **no
coordination at all**:

| rival_ratio | attempts | success | believed p | realised |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.0 | 1.00 | **1.00** | 1.000 | 1.000 |
| 0.5 | 1.00 | **1.00** | 1.000 | 1.000 |
| 0.8 | 1.00 | **1.00** | 0.984 | 1.000 |
| 1.0 | 0.72 | 0.50 | 0.692 | 0.690 |
| 1.2 | 0.12 | **0.05** | 0.544 | 0.400 |
| 1.5 | **0.00** | **0.00** | — | — |
| 2.0 | **0.00** | **0.00** | — | — |

**A single rival at 1.2× suppresses grabs almost entirely; at 1.5× the mover never
even tries.** Under pooling, a rival at parity (1.0×) is already enough for zero
attempts.

So the answer to your question is yes, and the mechanism is sharper than the RFC's
framing: when coordination is imperfect, deterrence is governed by the **maximum**
of the response distribution, not its sum. One credible near-peer substitutes for
the entire collective. That is a far cheaper immune system than §6's pooled levy
and it should probably become its own component in the H25–H29 stage.

The flip side is how much the v1 results depended on the coordination assumption:

| response rule | success |
|---|---|
| sum (perfect pooling, v1) | 0.33 |
| max (no coordination) | **1.00** |
| top-2 / top-3 / top-5 | **1.00** |

Every partial-pooling rule gives 1.00. That is *not* a bug — it is the `cap_scale`
pinning (§0.1 of v1): the mover is calibrated to beat the *full* pool, so any
partial pool loses by construction. But it means **§7's "the collective must
coordinate inside τ" is not one premise among three. It is the premise that carries
the entire result.** Lose perfect pooling and cooperation collapses even with high
δ, absorbing ruin and σ > 0.

## Q1c. The spectrum

`cap_spread` is the lognormal sd of the classic population — the shape of the
capability spectrum. I also now log spectrum statistics per run: top-1 share,
top1/top2 ratio, Gini, effective N (1/HHI).

| cap_spread | success (sum) | success (max) |
|---|---|---|
| 0.1 | 0.45 | 1.00 |
| 0.3 | 0.33 | 1.00 |
| 0.6 | 0.23 | 1.00 |
| 1.0 | **0.05** | 0.93 |

**Flagged confound — do not over-read the `sum` column.** A lognormal's mean is
`exp(σ²/2)`, so raising `cap_spread` raises *total* pool capability as well as
inequality, while the mover stays pinned to the reference cell's spread of 0.3.
Part of that 0.45 → 0.05 fall is simply a richer pool, not a shape effect. Needs
re-running with the total normalised before I'd claim a spectrum result under
pooling. The `max` column is clean (shape only reaches the outcome through the top
agent) and shows a mild effect in the opposite direction: a wider spectrum means a
bigger top agent, hence slightly more deterrence.

`cap_scale`, now a first-class axis as you asked — this is the knife edge:

| cap_scale | attempts | success |
|---|---|---|
| 0.5 | 0.00 | 0.00 |
| 0.8 | 0.10 | 0.03 |
| 1.0 | 0.62 | 0.33 |
| 1.3 | 1.00 | 0.88 |
| 1.6 | 1.00 | 1.00 |
| 3.0 | 1.00 | 1.00 |

The whole transition happens between 0.8× and 1.6×. Outside that band the model
is saturated and tells you nothing — which is why v1's first three attempts read
all-zeros. **Any future sweep must sit inside this band**, and every v1 number was
taken at exactly 1.0, i.e. the most sensitive point in the space.

## Q3. Decision-maker distributions among the initial competitors

EV is now the default, per your lean. Mixed populations, under **no coordination**
(the pooled case is degenerate — see caveat below):

| population | attempts | success | believed p | realised | calib gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 × EV | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 0.000 |
| 4 × EV | 1.02 | 1.00 | 0.710 | 0.976 | −0.266 |
| 4 × impatient (δ=0.5) | 1.02 | **1.00** | 0.705 | 0.976 | −0.271 |
| 2 × EV + 2 × threshold | 0.80 | 0.78 | 0.729 | 0.969 | −0.240 |
| 4 × threshold (θ=0.9) | 0.55 | 0.55 | 0.828 | 1.000 | −0.172 |
| 2 × impatient + 2 × patient | 0.75 | 0.68 | 0.696 | 0.900 | −0.204 |
| 4 × patient (δ=0.999) | **0.28** | **0.28** | 0.730 | 1.000 | −0.270 |

Three things worth your attention:

1. **Composition is a real lever.** All-impatient 1.00 vs all-patient 0.28, same
   capability, same everything else. Mixing them lands in between (0.68) rather
   than the patient ones restraining the impatient — the impatient move first and
   the population composition just sets *how soon* someone does.
2. **Threshold agents are more cautious than EV agents** (0.55 vs 1.00), which is
   an artifact of my parameters, not a finding: at G = L = 100 the EV condition
   is satisfied near p > 0.5, while θ = 0.9 demands near-certainty. Worth noting
   because it means "EV vs threshold" is not a clean dial — the comparison is
   entirely about where G/L puts the implied threshold.
3. **Calibration flips sign** (now −0.17 to −0.27, i.e. *under*confidence). The
   mover marginalises over a pooled response because the rule is public, but the
   realised response is a max. Movers systematically *underestimate* their odds
   and still win. A collective that publishes a pooling rule it cannot actually
   execute is bluffing, and the bluff is what it is measuring.

Patience, cleanly, single mover under pooling:

| δ | attempts (σ_self=0) | attempts (σ_self=0.3) |
|---|---|---|
| 0.5 | 0.75 | 0.93 |
| 0.9 | 0.62 | 0.80 |
| 0.99 | 0.12 | 0.15 |
| 0.999 | **0.07** | 0.15 |

H2 holds and is the most robust single effect in either sweep. Self-noise raises
attempts at *every* δ, so the two mechanisms are independent, not substitutes.

**Caveat on the pooled multi-mover cells.** Under `sum`, any population with ≥2
movers gives **zero** attempts at every `cap_scale` from 1.0 to 3.0. That is
self-referential deterrence: each mover counts the other movers' (large)
capability as part of the collective's response capacity. I hit this in v1 and
mistook it for a result. It is arguably realistic — rival movers *are* part of
what deters you — but it is doing so much work that mixed-population conclusions
cannot be drawn from the pooled arm at all. Needs an explicit model of whether
movers resist each other's grabs. **This is now the top open question.**
