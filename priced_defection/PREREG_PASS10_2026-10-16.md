## Definitions (how the switches act on the engine)

- A takeover resolves in `resolve_grabs6`. The mover wins the contest iff its capability exceeds the response, where the response is the `response_rule` combination (sum, max, top-k) of the capabilities of every agent that detected it AND holds a paid posture.
- **Gates.** With m gates the mover must pass m checks. Gate 0 is the existing detector set. Each further gate draws its own detectors afresh: every other living agent with a covering posture detects independently with the existing detection probability. The mover passes iff its capability exceeds the LARGEST response over the m gates. With probability `gate_dep` a gate reuses gate 0's detector set instead (a shared dependency: one vendor, one registry). `gates=1` is the existing engine.
- **Channel.** Each detector's alarm reaches the response pool with probability `1 - chan_d * (1 - chan_q)`. d is the chance the public channel is corrupted. q is the chance a protected channel carries the alarm anyway.
- All new randomness comes from a separate generator seeded from the run seed, so the main random stream is untouched when a switch is off.
- The mover's own beliefs are NOT updated for degradation or extra gates. It decides as in pass 9. So attempt counts should not change; outcomes should. A mover that knew and adapted (the realistic case) would attempt more under degradation. This understates the effect and is a known limit.

## Two corrections to what I said earlier today (kept in the file on purpose)

1. **The d x q grid is one-dimensional by construction.** Delivery probability depends only on e = d(1-q). Sweeping d and q separately would restate that one parameter. Pass 10 therefore sweeps e. The interesting version of q, where the protected channel reaches only authenticated members and cannot bootstrap, belongs to pass 11 (clubs).
2. **My earlier "correction" of the fracture prediction mixed up two switches.** In the 2x2 write-ups, `sum` and `max` label `frontier_rule` (the win bar the mover must clear to be decisive). The response is `response_rule`, which was `sum` in every 2x2 cell. The sum-frontier zeros in the 2x2 come from the win bar, which alarm delivery does not touch. So channel degradation should leave sum-frontier cells at zero. The original prediction (pooled responses degrade more than max-style responses) was about `response_rule`, and it stands. I had it right, then talked myself out of it.

## Predictions

Cells: calibrated cells are chosen by a short declared calibration run (not scored) and then fixed before the scored run. The calibration targets a gates=1, d=0 takeover rate between 0.2 and 0.8, so effects have room to move. 20 seeds per cell, bootstrap CIs.

**T1 (identity, must pass before any sweep).** (a) gates=1 and d=0 reproduce pass 9 seed-for-seed on both budget paths. (b) gates=3 with gate_dep=1 equals gates=1 exactly. (c) d>0 with q=1 equals d=0 exactly. (d) determinism. Failure of any of these voids the run.

**T2 (independent gates, monotone).** In a calibrated cell, takeover rate is non-increasing in m over m in {1, 2, 3, 5}, and m=5 is below m=1 outside the confidence intervals. Falsified if m=5 is within the CI of m=1.

**T3 (shared dependency collapses the gates).** At m=5, takeover rate with gate_dep=1 equals m=1 exactly (by T1b); with gate_dep=0.5 it lies between the independent and the shared values. The interesting test is the middle one. Falsified if gate_dep=0.5 is not between the two, outside CI.

**T4 (ceiling).** In the saturated 2x2 cell (cheap capability, max frontier, no sharing, takeover 1.00 at gates=1) takeover stays at or above 0.90 at m=5. If it falls, the 3x lead was not saturating and the 2x2 reading changes.

**T5 (degradation raises takeover).** In a calibrated max-frontier cell, takeover rate is non-decreasing in e over {0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0}, and at e=1 (no alarm delivered) it equals the takeover rate of a run with no posture response at all, up to CI.

**T6 (sum-style responses lose more than max-style).** Measure the mean ratio of delivered response to undegraded response, per resolved takeover. Prediction: at e=0.5, the ratio under `response_rule=sum` is close to 0.5 and under `response_rule=max` is clearly above 0.5, because the maximum survives random loss of alarms when several detectors are present. Falsified if the max ratio is within 0.05 of the sum ratio.

**T7 (sum-frontier cells are flat in e).** In the 2x2 sum-frontier cells (takeover 0.00 at e=0) takeover stays 0.00 at every e. If it moves, the win bar is not the only reason for those zeros and the 2x2 C2 reading is wrong.

**T8 (attempts do not move).** Mean attempts per run do not change with m or e beyond CI, because the mover is unaware of both. If they do move, the beliefs are leaking through some path I did not intend, and that is a bug to find.

## Not being tested here

- Whether a real mover would adapt to degradation or extra gates.
- Part C (raiding fallback, misattribution, autoimmunity).
- Anything about real elections, cyber defence or the G7 exercises. The gate result is a statement about independence versus a shared dependency inside this model.

## What would change our minds

- T2 and T3 both false: independence of checks is not what protects the order in this model, and the "complexity as a guardrail" analogy loses its mechanism.
- T4 false: the saturated cells are not saturated and the pass 8 and 2x2 readings need re-running.
- T7 false: the 2x2 explanation for the sum-frontier zeros is wrong.
