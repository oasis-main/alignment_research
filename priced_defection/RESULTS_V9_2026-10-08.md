## 1. The S4 anomaly is SOLVED — and it was not what I guessed

Pass 8 left this open: against a hard access gate with cheap capability,
public sharing *reduced* the leader's lead (3.87 → 3.42) yet *raised* endgame
probability (0.90 → 1.00). My pass-8 hypothesis was frontier compression. A
probe falsified that before I built this pass, and pointed at budget
competition instead. **The probe was also wrong.** The decisive test:

| cell | endgame | posture share | n_post | n_share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| no sharing | 0.90 | **0.779** | 331.3 | 0.0 |
| sharing, shared purse | 1.00 | **0.015** | 3.3 | 157.1 |
| sharing, **separate ring-fenced purse** | 1.00 | **0.020** | 4.3 | 151.0 |
| sharing available but **priced out of reach** | 0.90 | **0.779** | 331.3 | 0.0 |

Ring-fencing the money changes nothing (0.015 → 0.020). Making sharing
*unaffordable* restores defence **exactly** to the no-sharing baseline
(0.779, n_post 331.3). So the scarce resource was never the budget:

> **The binding constraint is the one-action-per-round slot, not money.**
> Agents in this model have one *decision* per round. A diffusion programme
> does not out-*spend* defence, it out-*competes* it for the agent's single
> action. Ring-fencing a budget cannot fix a scarcity of agency.

Confirmed by the endowment sweep (N3), which is monotone in exactly the wrong
direction for a money story — a *larger* sharing endowment buys *less*
posture, because it means more rounds spent sharing:

| `share_endow` | posture share | n_share | endgame | lead 3.00 → |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.05 | **0.622** | 33.9 | 1.00 | 3.53 |
| 0.15 | 0.273 | 99.4 | 1.00 | 3.45 |
| 0.25 | 0.017 | 145.7 | 1.00 | 3.42 |
| 0.50 | 0.017 | 145.7 | 1.00 | 3.42 |

This is a better finding than the one I predicted, and it generalises beyond
the model: **an institution with one unit of attention cannot run a diffusion
programme and a defence posture simultaneously, however well funded it is.**
Money is fungible; attention is not. Our pass 6–8 results all assumed one
action per round without ever testing whether that assumption was load-bearing.
It is. **Declared as the single largest untested structural assumption in the
whole study**, and it is now first on the next list.

### T2 CONFIRMED — and it bounds the anomaly

Under `frontier_rule="sum"` the anomaly does not occur at all:

| frontier rule | no sharing | with sharing |
|---|---|---|
| `max` | endgame 0.90, lead 3.87 | endgame **1.00**, lead 3.42 |
| `sum` | endgame **0.00** | endgame **0.00** |

So the anomaly requires a frontier set by a *single strongest defender*. When
the backup stock pools, diffusion is unambiguously good and the endgame never
arrives. **The danger case is specifically: one defender carries the frontier,
and diffusion consumes that defender's attention.**

## 2. T1 FAILED (ring-fencing), and the failure is the result

Stated plainly: I predicted a ring-fenced purse would restore posture and push
the endgame below baseline. It did neither (0.014 → 0.017; endgame stays 1.00).
The policy reading matters — **"fund diffusion separately so it doesn't
cannibalise defence" does not work in this model.** You must either give
agents more actions, or accept the trade.

## 3. Calling a bluff — T3 and T4 both FAILED

### BUG FOUND: my precision number was my own bookkeeping

First reported figures showed calling at precision **0.08** — 92% false
accusations — and I nearly wrote that up as "verification is noisy." It was my
scoring. Concurrent callers of the *same* bluffer all resolved after the first
call had already collapsed the bluff, so every duplicate was scored a false
accusation. Fixed by snapshotting truth before any call resolves, and counting
duplication separately as `calls_redundant`.

| | before fix | after fix |
|---|---|---|
| precision | **0.08** | **1.00** |
| right / wrong | 83 / 1041 | **1122 / 1.5** |

Verification is *accurate*. What it is, is **massively duplicated** — ~6.4
calls per round across 20 agents, all testing the same prominent claim. That
is the real phenomenon, and it is a different thing from being wrong:
uncoordinated verifiers all check the most prominent claim and nobody checks
anything else.

### T3 FAILED — verification is NOT a public good *as I built it*

I predicted calling would collapse to ~zero at `call_bounty=0`, matching
`post_shield` and `rep_value`, giving a third instance of one invariant.
Instead it is heavily provided *at zero bounty*:

| `call_bounty` | calls/run | callers (of 20) | precision | bluff share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0.0** | **754.9** | **15.7** | 1.00 | 0.824 |
| 0.05 | 860.7 | 17.0 | 1.00 | 0.824 |
| 0.3 | 1137.5 | 19.3 | 0.99 | 0.827 |
| 0.6 | 1265.4 | 19.9 | 1.00 | 0.832 |

**The reason is my own design, and I should have seen it when I wrote it:** I
gave `call` a *private relief* term — exposing a bluff removes deterrence that
was fooling the caller personally. That is a genuine private return, so `call`
was never a pure public good. The honest statement is not "the invariant
failed" but **"I did not test the invariant"** — I built a private benefit into
the action and then predicted public-good behaviour from it.

The three-instance claim is therefore **withdrawn, not refuted.** Testing it
needs a variant where exposure corrects *others'* beliefs but not the
caller's. That is a clean experiment and it is on the list.

What the price sweep does show is sane: calling responds to its own price
(1249 at `c_call=0.02` → 24.2 at 0.6), so the branch is price-sensitive even
though it is over-provided.

### T4 FAILED — verification does not kill bluffing

The prediction was that active verification collapses bluffing and returns
real growth. It does not, and this is robust across every cell:

| regime | bluff share | apparent/true | n_grow | endgame |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| bluffing, no verification | 0.815 | 1.410 | 0.0 | 0.03 |
| + verification, bounty 0.0 | 0.824 | 1.418 | **0.0** | 0.03 |
| + verification, bounty 0.3 | 0.827 | 1.420 | **0.0** | 0.03 |
| + verification, bounty 0.6 | 0.832 | 1.423 | **0.0** | 0.03 |

~1,100 accurate exposures per run, and bluffing is *unchanged* — in fact
marginally higher. Real growth stays at exactly zero. Even with callers'
priors swept from 0.1 to 0.9 (block Q2), nothing shifts.

**Mechanism: a bluff is cheap and re-buyable; an exposure is per-instance.**
Collapsing one bluff does not make the *next* bluff unattractive, so
verification becomes a treadmill — perfectly accurate, perfectly futile. To
deter bluffing you would need the *penalty for being caught* to scale with
being caught repeatedly, which this model does not implement (`bluff_caught`
is a flat reputation hit and reputation decays).

### T5 CONFIRMED — the inspection lag protects the bluffer

| `pi_call` (lag) | calls/run | bluff share | precision |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1796.5 | 0.829 | 0.99 |
| 2 | 1137.5 | 0.827 | 0.99 |
| 4 | 522.1 | 0.819 | **0.33** |
| 8 | 211.5 | 0.817 | 0.99 |

Bluff share is flat (0.829 → 0.817) across an eight-fold change in inspection
speed. The bluffer is always ahead by the lag. And longer bluffs are *more*
attractive, not less (S2: `bluff_len=10` gives bluff share 0.906, apparent/true
1.459) — the deterrence outlasts the inspection.

*(The `pi_call=4` precision dip to 0.33 is a real residual-duplication effect
at that lag, not the old bug; declared, not swept.)*

## 4. BUG FOUND IN MY OWN PASS-8 SAFEGUARD

Pass 8's contribution was a price-calibration gate asserting every action is
chosen somewhere as its price falls. **It passed `call` as "ok" while `call`
was being chosen 750–1265 times per run by 16–20 of 20 agents.** The gate was
one-sided: it caught *dead* branches and was blind to *runaway* ones.

I added a two-sided version (`price_calibration_2sided`) testing both tails
plus a monopolisation cap. **It also passes `call` as "ok"** — `call` responds
to its own price, so it is not price-blind, and its 31.8% share of all acts
sits under my 90% cap.

So: **the safeguard is still insufficient, and I am reporting that rather than
tuning the threshold post-hoc to catch this one case.** What the pass-9
evidence actually says is that the diagnostic I need is not about prices at
all, but about *action-slot share* — the S4 mechanism. A branch consuming 30%+
of every agent's decisions in a 7-action menu is distorting the model even at
a sane price. Designing that gate properly is next, and it must be designed
against the mechanism rather than fitted to this example.

## What pass 9 changes about what we may claim

1. **The S4 anomaly is resolved: action-slot scarcity, not budget.** Diffusion
   competes with defence for *attention*, not money. Ring-fencing funds does
   not help. This supersedes both my pass-8 hypothesis and my own probe.
2. **One action per round is now known to be load-bearing**, and it was never
   tested across passes 6–8. Largest untested structural assumption in the
   study.
3. **The anomaly needs a single-defender frontier.** Under a pooled frontier,
   diffusion is unambiguously good.
4. **Verification is accurate but duplicated and futile here.** Perfect
   precision, ~1,100 exposures/run, zero effect on bluffing, because bluffs
   are re-buyable and exposure is per-instance.
5. **The inspection lag structurally protects the overstater.** Bluff share is
   invariant to an eight-fold change in verification speed.
6. **The three-instance public-good invariant is withdrawn, not refuted** — I
   priced a private return into `call` and then predicted public-good
   behaviour. My error, not the model's.
7. **My pass-8 safeguard is insufficient and its replacement is too.** The
   right diagnostic is action-slot share, not price response.

## Next, in order

1. **Multiple actions per round** (`actions_per_round`), and re-run the pass
   6–8 headlines under it. If the public-good collapse or the price-regime
   result moves, several conclusions need restating. This is first because it
   is the assumption everything else rests on.
2. **Action-slot-share diagnostic**, designed against the S4 mechanism.
3. **Pure-public-good `call`** — exposure corrects others' beliefs only — to
   actually test the withdrawn invariant.
4. **Escalating penalty for repeated exposure**, the missing ingredient that
   would let verification deter bluffing rather than chase it.
5. Still open from pass 6: the upper-bound claim in a region where response
   capacity binds rather than backups.
