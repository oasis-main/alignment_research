# Pass 7 results — one currency, asymmetric prices, diffusion, bluffing (2026-10-08)

**Pre-registered predictions Q1–Q5 are in the `tournament7.py` docstring,
written before the first run.** Of the five: one partially confirmed, **two
failed outright**, one split in a way I did not anticipate, and one is **void
on a scaling bug of my own**. The failure of Q2 is the most useful result in
the study, because it means pass 6's headline survived its main threat.

Harness: `tournament7.py`, 30 seeds/cell, N=20, 200 rounds, mc=32, stdlib
only, every run seeded. `roles="assigned"` reproduces pass 5 seed-for-seed;
features-off + `repriced=False` reproduces pass 6 seed-for-seed. Selftest
PASS, including non-rivalry, bluff-affects-apparent-only, and club-gating
checks.

Raw: `raw/results_v7_{F..J}.json`, `raw/sweep_v7_{F..J}.txt`.

## 0. The currency fix (the reason pass 7 exists)

Pass 6 reported `seize` never chosen at any price and `prep` chosen ~274×/run,
and I declared it a unit error rather than a tuning error. Diagnosed exactly:

| Fault | Effect |
|---|---|
| PD payoffs fed the budget `b`; the takeover prize went only to `score` | `prep`'s payoff was **invisible to the budget constraint** that priced everything else |
| `seize` paid a 20.00 sanction for a prize of 0.75 | always negative — never chosen, at any price |
| Neither branch discounted by delay; `prep` paid no sanction at all | `prep` dominated by construction |

v7 puts every branch in budget units, present-valued at the decision round,
and makes the prize **endogenous**: a decisive win captures the other agents'
capability, valued at the same `lambda` that prices growth, defence and raids.
That removes the exogenous `G` from the valuation — one price for capability,
used everywhere.

**Effect of the fix alone** (block F, vs pass 6 matched): `prep` falls from
~274 to **8.1** per run, and posture provision rises from 0.185 to **0.974**.
The model was not mildly mispriced; aggression was roughly 30× over-weighted.
Every pass-6 behavioural count should be read as void, not just blocks D/E.

`prize_rule` matters and is now the main declared assumption: endogenous gives
endgame 0.07, exogenous 0.00 (F3).

## Q1. Partially confirmed — the comparison is fixed, `seize` is still dominated

`seize` revives only marginally: 0.0 → **0.4** per run in the most favourable
cell (`c_seize=0.05`, `take_frac=0.4`, `transfer_mult=1.5`), and 0.0 at the
default take. It is no longer *structurally impossible* — the selftest
confirms it fires — but `prep` still dominates it across block F, and
**`c_seize` has no effect on any outcome** (F: endgame 0.07 at every price
from 0.05 to 1.5).

Honest reading: I fixed the unit error and `seize` is still not a live
margin. Either partial raiding is genuinely dominated by full takeover in this
parameter region, or `prep` remains over-valued by a smaller factor. I am not
claiming the former. **The prep/seize margin is still not a trustworthy
instrument**, and any result that depends on it stays void.

## Q2. FAILED — and this is the important one

I predicted asymmetric prices would **kill** pass 6's erosion mechanism: if
the leader is also the cheapest buyer, cheap capability should stop diffusing
to laggards and the lead should lock in. **It did not happen.**

First, the control replicates pass 6's P3 *under the new currency* (G1):

| lead | `c_grow` | endgame | lead start → end |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3.0 | 0.1 (cheap) | 0.00 [0.00,0.00] | 3.00 → **1.37** |
| 3.0 | 0.6 (dear) | **1.00 [1.00,1.00]** | 3.00 → **3.00** |

Then the realistic case — leader pays less per unit (G2, `lead0=3.0`):

| `price_scale` | cheap (`c_grow=0.1`) | dear (`c_grow=0.6`) |
|---|---|---|
| 0.00 (flat) | END 0.00, lead → 1.37 | END 1.00, lead → 3.00 |
| 0.25 | END 0.03, lead → **1.40** | END 1.00, lead → 3.03 |
| 0.50 | END 0.03, lead → **1.39** | END 1.00, lead → 3.13 |
| 1.00 | END 0.03, lead → **1.47** | END 0.90, lead → **3.92** |

Erosion survives economies of scale almost untouched (1.37 → 1.47 as the
leader's discount goes from none to full). **The regime split is driven by the
absolute price level of capability, not by the relative price between
incumbent and entrant.** Pass 6's most striking result survived the attack I
designed to break it, which raises my confidence in it materially.

The one real effect of asymmetry is in the *dear* column: at `price_scale=1.0`
the leader's lead **grows to 3.92** rather than holding at 3.00. So scale
economies intensify lock-in where lock-in already happens, and barely matter
where diffusion already happens. Asymmetry is a second-order modifier of a
first-order price effect.

Caveat I want on the record: `price_scale` here is a *smooth* function of
relative size. Real access asymmetry is closer to a step — you have the
weights or you do not. A step function is the obvious next test and could
behave differently.

## Q3. Split — public collapses (as predicted), **club collapses too** (not predicted)

Replicating pass 6's posture result on a second, independent mechanism:

| scope | `c_share` | shares/run | sharers (of 20) |
|---|---|---|---|
| public | 0.05 / 0.2 / 0.5 | **0.0 / 0.0 / 0.0** | 0 |
| club | 0.05 / 0.2 / 0.5 | **0.0 / 0.0 / 0.0** | 0 |

Public sharing collapsing to exactly zero at every price **confirms** the
public-good result on a mechanism built independently of `post`. That is now
two for two, and I treat it as the most robust finding in the study.

But **club sharing collapsed too, which I predicted it would not**, and the
reason is structural rather than incidental: the club starts empty. With
`rep=0` for everyone and `club_thresh=1.0`, there are no members, so there is
no inflow to value, so nobody pays to share, so the club never forms. **A
reputation club cannot bootstrap itself** — the private return it offers is
*created by* the participation it requires. That is a genuine finding about
trust-gated cooperation and it was invisible to me when I designed the test.

What rescues sharing is a direct private return to reputation (H2):

| `rep_value` | shares/run | sharers | lead 1.25 → | endgame |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.0 | 0.0 | 0 | 1.29 | 0.07 |
| 0.1 | **0.0** | 0 | 1.29 | 0.07 |
| 0.3 | **303.9** | **20** | **1.02** | 0.00 |
| 0.6 | 3061.4 | 20 | **1.00** | 0.00 |

Same bimodal shape as the posture shield, threshold between 0.1 and 0.3:
nobody, then everybody. **Reputation is the mechanism that makes diffusion
privately rational**, and once it crosses threshold, capability equalises
almost completely (lead → 1.00).

## Q4. Not supported as stated — but the underlying question answered clearly

I predicted club sharing would produce a two-tier world: lead eroding over
members, gap to non-members widening. My club/non-club instrument does not
show that (I2: leader is 3.32× club members vs 3.14× non-members — the wrong
direction), and the instrument is **confounded**: club membership requires
paying to share, so members spend budget that non-members keep. I am not
drawing a two-tier conclusion from it.

The question Mike actually asked — *is public diffusion better than a trust
club?* — is answered unambiguously by total movement and final lead, at a 3×
starting lead (I2/I3):

| regime | capability moved | lead 3.00 → | endgame |
|---|---|---|---|
| no sharing | 0.00 | **3.12** | **1.00 [1.00,1.00]** |
| club sharing | 30.23 | 2.75 | 0.73 |
| **public sharing** | **67.93** | **1.21** | **0.00** |

And against the *dear-capability lock-in cell* specifically (`c_grow=0.6`,
the regime the news looks like) (I3):

| regime | lead 3.00 → | endgame |
|---|---|---|
| no sharing | 3.00 | **1.00** |
| club sharing | 2.65 | 0.80 |
| **public sharing** | **1.21** | **0.00** |

**Public diffusion moves more than twice the capability of a trust club and
takes the endgame from certain to never.** It is the only intervention in
seven passes that defeats the dear-capability lock-in. Mike's intuition that
"there is something to be said for sharing publicly to benefit everyone" is
supported here about as strongly as this model can support anything — with the
essential proviso that it only happens at all if reputation pays.

Mechanism, stated plainly: sharing is **non-rival** (the sharer keeps its
capability; selftest checks this) and **catch-up weighted** (it lifts only
those behind). So it raises the frontier that a takeover must clear without
costing the sharer anything real. Raiding redistributes; sharing expands.
Those are different operations and the model now distinguishes them.

*(Note: the `c_grow=0.3` and `c_grow=0.6` public cells are numerically
identical because once sharing dominates, growth is never purchased, so
`c_grow` has no effect. Expected, not a bug.)*

## Q5. VOID — bluffing is mispriced, by me, again

Bluffing is chosen in exactly **one cell of eleven** (`c_bluff=0.02`,
`bluff_mult=1.5`: bluff-rounds 0.537, apparent/true capability **1.803**) and
essentially never otherwise. The cause is a scaling error in my valuation: I
divided the deterrence annuity by `cfg["rounds"]`, crushing the payoff by
~200×. The narrow affordability window (0.537 at `c_bluff=0.02` → 0.001 at
0.1) is the signature of a value term that is nearly always below price.

So **Q5 is untested and block J is void.** I am not reporting a substitution
result off one cell.

The one suggestive observation, flagged as suggestive only: in that cell the
world looks **80% stronger than it is** (app 1.803) while endgame probability
is unchanged (0.07 vs 0.07) and `t_end` is slightly later (76 vs 69). That is
the shape my prediction expected — apparent stability diverging from true
stability — but one cell on a known-broken price is not evidence. Fix and
re-run before anyone repeats this.

This is the second consecutive pass where **my own price vector**, not the
mechanism, was the binding error. That is now the standing risk in this
codebase and it deserves a structural fix: a price-calibration selftest that
asserts each action is chosen at least once across a declared price sweep.

## What pass 7 changes about what we may claim

1. **Pass 6's price-regime result is real and robust.** It survived the
   currency fix *and* the asymmetric-price attack. The absolute price of
   capability selects the regime; incumbent discounts only intensify lock-in
   where it already occurs.
2. **The public-good collapse is now two-for-two** on independently built
   mechanisms (defence, diffusion). Voluntary provision of a non-excludable
   good is zero, not small.
3. **Reputation clubs cannot bootstrap.** Trust-gated sharing never starts
   from an empty club. This was not predicted and is the most interesting
   mechanism discovered this pass.
4. **Public diffusion beats a trust club, and beats lock-in.** Non-rival,
   catch-up-weighted sharing is the only lever found so far that takes a
   certain endgame to zero.
5. **Our pass-6 behavioural counts are void**, not just blocks D/E —
   aggression was ~30× over-weighted before the fix.
6. **My prices remain the largest error source.** Two passes running.

## Next, in order

1. **Price-calibration selftest** — assert every action is chosen somewhere in
   a declared price sweep. This would have caught both the pass-6 and pass-7
   failures automatically.
2. Reprice `bluff` (drop the spurious `/rounds`) and re-run J.
3. Step-function access asymmetry instead of smooth `price_scale`.
4. Reputation club seeded with founding members, to separate "cannot
   bootstrap" from "cannot sustain".
5. The still-unproven pass-6 gap: re-test the upper-bound claim in a region
   where response capacity binds, not backups.
